Studio work plan.

Mend the Pattern turns a finite material supply into a drawing rule. A tear produces the entire repair budget. When the viewer changes stitch placement, the cloth and its budget stay fixed so the consequences of that choice are visible.

Group brief

Use SDG 12 and target 12.5, which calls for reducing waste through prevention, reduction, recycling and reuse. Confirm the quote, paragraph and action as a group. Each member builds an individual artwork around the shared message. United Nations, Goal 12

“The economy is entirely dependent on finite materials.”

Ellen MacArthur, sailor and circular economy advocate. Ellen MacArthur Foundation, The circular economy at sea

Draft paragraph for the group to revise

At sea, Ellen MacArthur learned to work with the supplies she had. Her observation connects to SDG 12 because repairing an object keeps materials in use and can prevent waste. In this textile, each repair spends the thread its tear leaves behind. Before you replace something this week, try one repair with a material you already have and show someone how you did it.

Build the edition

Flowerseed informed the black stage, soft glow and use of bright accent colors. The p5.js artwork uses an original weave and repair model. Flowerseed

Start with a garment you own

Photograph a worn cuff, bag or other fabric you would consider discarding. Sketch its repeat pattern, the direction of its threads and where it wears thin. Translate one observation into a drawing rule, such as shorter fibers toward the fold. Use the photograph and sketches in your design document.

Make the constraint visible

  1. Weave. Use nested loops for rows and columns and a conditional to alternate the direction of strokes. Set randomSeed() so you can reproduce an edition. Use your garment study to choose the repeat motif and one change in fiber length or density.
  2. Tear and recover. Use the seed to choose a horizontal or vertical tear and a starting position. Trace two irregular edges along that axis. Sum the lengths of the salvaged fibers to get the same available thread for both repairs of a given tear.
  3. Spend. Give each possible stitch a cost equal to its path length rounded up plus a knot allowance. For Spread, visit candidate positions across all 12 seam bands. For Concentrate, start at the widest part and work within a local window. Use the same stitch costs and recovered length for both repairs. Skip any stitch that would overspend.
  4. Print. Use the seed to pick a palette and a motif, such as diamonds, herringbone or ripples. Draw each fiber and pass two bands of light across the cloth. Give the repair a contrasting accent and soften the light with a halo. Use noLoop() for a still image with controls to compare editions.

Check every edition
used thread + thread left = recovered thread
A band counts as reached when at least one stitch crosses it.

Start with a small grid and one repair. Confirm the accounting before adding texture or the second placement rule. The preview uses p5.js for drawing and ordinary JavaScript loops, arrays, conditionals and functions for the model. p5.js library

Make the sounds

Record a single clean take of a piece of fabric you own. Scrunch it, draw a thread along it, and tap the folded edge, leaving a pause between gestures. Build all three clips from that take. Keep the raw file and a short edit log with the time ranges and effects you used.

  • Fray. Cut several short grains from the scrunch and arrange them in an uneven burst about half a second long. Put the loudest grain at the point corresponding to the widest part of the tear.
  • Pull through. Use a short thread scrape or fabric tap as the pulse. Place each pulse in a one-to-two-second timeline using its stitch’s vertical position. Spread will sound spaced out. Concentrate will sound like a tighter cluster.
  • Hold. Slow and layer two fragments of the fabric tap to make a softer one-second sound. Let the ends decay. Use it when the final balance appears in a later animated version.

Export fray.wav, pull-through.wav and hold.wav. Listen to each clip through headphones and a phone speaker. The web preview uses synthesized placeholders. Make the three submitted sounds from your own recording.

Test the idea

Each page load generates a different edition. To replay an example, enter 1205 in the Weave seed field and select Go. Its 1,629 units reach 11 of 12 bands with Spread and 2 of 12 with Concentrate.

  1. Try six seeds. Within each seed, compare the two rules and check that recovered length stays fixed, the arithmetic balances, and the repairs visibly differ.
  2. Show three to five classmates the two repairs before showing the paragraph. Ask what stayed the same and what each repair did with the thread. After they read the page, ask them to name the action they could take.
  3. Aim for four of five viewers to recognize the shared budget and name the repair action. If people miss the shared budget, put the recovered count beside the strategy control. If people miss the action, move its sentence closer to the cloth.

Production schedule

  1. Session 1 · Group decisions and solo sketches. Finalize the quote, paragraph and action. Collect your garment observation and sketch an unbroken repeat, an open tear and a possible join. Pick the strongest repeat for the prototype.
  2. Session 2 · Model and artwork. Build the cloth and tear, log the recovered length, then implement the repair rules. Choose one seed where the placements read clearly. Finish the art and web typography after the data checks pass.
  3. Session 3 · Original audio and critique. Record and edit the three clips. Run the viewer test and one mobile check. Revise the stitch contrast, budget display or action based on what viewers actually miss.
  4. Session 4 · Documentation and export. Save your p5.js source, web page, three WAV files and screenshots showing both repairs of one seed and a second seed. Include your artwork rules, the group paragraph, garment sketches, findings from critique and audio process in the design document. Cite any adapted example by URL in a nearby code comment and in the design document, with a note on how you developed it.

Extend the same edition later

For Assignment 2, reveal the tear, place each stitch in sequence and trigger your recording at each stage. For Assignment 3, let viewers choose bands to sew or unsew. Return thread to the budget when a stitch is undone so the material rule still holds.
